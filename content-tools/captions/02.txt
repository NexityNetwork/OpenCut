↓ Here is how the multi-model architecture actually works:
This is a complete breakdown of specialized AI models, moving away from relying on a single generalist model and instead assigning specific tasks to the engine best optimized for the job.

Application architecture and code execution: You start by splitting your frontend and backend development between specialized models built for those exact domains.

For the frontend, you route through Kimi-K3. It handles fluid UI components, modern styling logic, and complex state rendering without throwing layout errors or broken hooks.

For the backend, you hand the operational logic to FABLE. It builds the database schemas, configures your API endpoints, and structures serverless functions without leaking context or breaking dependencies.

When code breaks or throws silent runtime errors, you pass the stack trace to GPT-5.6 SOL. It acts as your dedicated debugging layer, isolating logical bugs and fixing race conditions instantly without rewriting existing functional code.

Media generation and asset production: For visual media, you pass prompts directly to GPT for image assets. It handles product UI mockups, promo tiles, and marketing visual generation with strict adherence to composition details.

For dynamic video assets and ad creatives, you route generation to Seedance 2.0. It turns static concepts into high-converting video scenes without requiring traditional video editing tools or manual timeline assembly.

Cost efficiency and heavy background processing: Running high-volume tasks through flagship models burns budget fast. You route high-token, repetitive operations to dedicated cost-optimized engines.

For bulk tasks, routine queries, and massive batch processing, you use DeepSeek V4 Pro. It delivers flagship-level execution at a fraction of the compute cost, keeping your overhead virtually non-existent.

For continuous background execution, you hand long-running tasks to GLM-5.2. It handles asynchronous workers, queue processing, data polling, and automated log processing quietly in the background without dropping requests.

The whole setup compounds: Every model plays to its specific strength. Your compute costs drop drastically by routing cheap tasks away from heavy models. Your engineering velocity scales because no single model gets overwhelmed.
