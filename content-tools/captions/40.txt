You do not need to build the perfect product.
You need to sell one that already works.

Most founders spend 12 months building, validating, iterating, and burning cash on an idea that might work.

This flips it: 
launch several proven products in 48 hours, watch what people actually buy, then scale only what converts.

Here is what changes:
Instead of building one thing and praying for traction, you test real demand across multiple products simultaneously. You see what people pay for, not what they say they want.

The products are already built. Draftboard, Trove, traxy.ai. Each one is production-ready software you can white-label or resell immediately.

You are not competing on innovation. 
You are competing on speed and distribution.
It does not matter if similar tools exist. Markets reward execution, not originality. 

If there is demand, there is room to capture a percentage of it. You do not need the whole market. You need a slice that pays consistently.

The shift: stop treating software like a craft project. Treat it like inventory. Stock what sells, drop what does not, double down on what converts.
